Essential Finance Reading List for Nepal

Timeless financial wisdom from classic literature, translated directly into practical realities for Nepali salary earners and investors.

Stage 1

Mindset, Psychology & Cash Flow Foundations

1. The Psychology of Money Morgan Housel

Doing well with money has a little to do with how smart you are and a lot to do with how you behave. Controlling your time and ego is the ultimate dividend of wealth.

Nepali Context Translation: In Nepal, family social comparison during Dashain, extravagant wedding feasts, and showing off expensive gadget EMIs are the #1 wealth killers. Housel’s rule: "Wealth is what you do not see"—the unbought car and unspent bonus.
🎯 Action: Establish an untouchable 6-month liquid emergency fund in a separate bank account before investing in stocks.
2. Rich Dad Poor Dad Robert Kiyosaki

The poor and middle class work for money; the rich have money work for them. Understand the crystal-clear difference between an asset and a liability.

Nepali Context Translation: Many Nepali families believe buying a financed car or heavy gold jewelry on high interest is an asset. Kiyosaki clarifies: If it takes money OUT of your pocket monthly (fuel, road tax, bank EMI), it is a liability. Focus first on cash-flowing assets.
🎯 Action: Calculate your Debt-to-Asset ratio using the risePaisa Cash Flow & Net Worth Tracker.
3. I Will Teach You To Be Rich Ramit Sethi

A 6-week automated personal finance system. Spend extravagantly on the things you truly love, as long as you ruthlessly cut costs on things you don't care about.

Nepali Context Translation: Automate your cash flow on the 1st of every Nepali month: When your salary hits your bank, immediately auto-transfer 20% to connectIPS mutual fund SIPs and emergency reserves before spending a single rupee on discretionary dining.
🎯 Action: Set up automated standing instructions on connectIPS for your monthly SIP deductions.
Stage 2

Wealth Compounding & Systemic Investing

4. The Little Book of Common Sense Investing John C. Bogle

Investing is about common sense. Don't look for the needle in the haystack; just buy the whole haystack. Minimizing management fees and trading frequency guarantees market-matching returns.

Nepali Context Translation: Most retail traders on NEPSE lose capital through hyperactive intraday churning and broker commissions. Bogle’s philosophy proves that a disciplined, low-expense open-ended mutual fund SIP consistently beats 80% of emotional traders over 10-year horizons.
🎯 Action: Allocate at least 50% of your equity portfolio into diversified mutual funds rather than individual hype stocks.
5. Your Money or Your Life Vicki Robin & Joe Dominguez

Money is something you trade your life energy for. Learn to calculate your real hourly wage and achieve financial independence through conscious frugality.

Nepali Context Translation: A Kathmandu job paying NPR 50,000/month often requires NPR 12,000 in commute/fuel, formal clothes, and daily restaurant lunch. Your "real wage" is much lower than your gross contract. Measure purchases in hours of life worked, not just rupees.
🎯 Action: Audit your monthly food delivery and restaurant bills against your actual hourly take-home wage.
Stage 3

Advanced Value Investing & NEPSE Mastery

6. The Intelligent Investor Benjamin Graham

The definitive book on value investing. The core pillar is 'Margin of Safety'—purchasing securities at a significant discount to their underlying intrinsic business value.

Nepali Context Translation: In NEPSE bull runs, speculative hydropower companies with negative reserve balances and heavy bank debt often surge due to market syndicates. Graham warns: When the bubble pops, only companies with strong cash earnings, low P/E, and high dividend yields survive.
🎯 Action: Never buy an individual stock without reviewing its quarterly EPS, Book Value, and Non-Performing Loan (NPL) ratio.
7. One Up On Wall Street Peter Lynch

How to use what you already know to make money in the market. Everyday consumers often spot great business opportunities long before institutional analysts.

Nepali Context Translation: Look around your everyday Nepali life: Which telecommunication networks do people rely on? Which commercial bank branches have lines out the door? Which consumer goods (soaps, teas, cement) dominate local stores? Invest in companies with tangible consumer stickiness.
🎯 Action: Create a watchlist of 5 listed companies whose products or services you personally use every single week.
8. Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits Philip Fisher

The 'Scuttlebutt Method' and 15 points to look for in a common stock. Focuses deeply on management integrity, R&D capability, and corporate governance.

Nepali Context Translation: Promoter integrity is the single most critical factor in Nepal’s capital markets. Examine AGM disclosures: Do promoters siphon capital into private subsidiary contracts? Are audited notes clean? A great business with dishonest promoters will eventually dilute retail shareholders.
🎯 Action: Read the Independent Auditor’s Report in the annual report before committing capital to any newly listed firm.
