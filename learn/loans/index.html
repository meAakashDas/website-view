Loans & Debt

Borrowing money can accelerate wealth creation through property acquisition or paralyze your household finances through compound interest traps. This path teaches you the critical mechanics of debt in Nepal: how banks calculate monthly EMIs using the reducing balance method, why flat-rate loans from cooperatives or auto dealers charge almost double the stated interest, how Base Rate + Premium fluctuates, and how to rapidly pay down debt using accelerated prepayment strategies.

1. What is this?

Definition & Concept

A loan is borrowed capital provided by a bank or financial institution that must be repaid over time with interest and service charges.

2. Why is it important?

Practical Importance

Taking a 20-year home loan in Nepal at 11% interest means you will repay more than double the original borrowed principal in interest alone. Understanding amortization saves millions of rupees.

3. How does it work in Nepal?

The Nepal Context & Regulatory Reality

Regulated by NRB. Banks must peg retail loan interest rates to their monthly published Base Rate with a fixed risk premium.

Glossary

Key Financial Terms in This Domain

Essential definitions and terminology you will encounter across lessons and guides:

Glossary

EMI (Equated Monthly Installment)

An Equated Monthly Installment (EMI) is a fixed monthly payment made by a borrower to a bank on a specific date, comprising both principal repayment and interest charges to clear a loan over time.
Glossary

DSTI (Debt-Service-to-Income Ratio)

The Debt-Service-to-Income (DSTI) ratio measures the percentage of your gross verified monthly income that goes toward paying monthly debt obligations, capped by Nepal Rastra Bank at a maximum of 50%.
Glossary

Reducing-Balance Interest Rate

A Reducing-Balance Interest Rate calculates interest solely on the remaining outstanding principal debt each month, shrinking interest charges as you repay.
FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Practical answers to common financial dilemmas faced by learners in Nepal.

Most retail loans in Nepal are issued on a floating rate pegged to the bank’s Base Rate. When bank deposit costs rise, the Base Rate increases, causing your overall loan rate to increase. Depending on your agreement, the bank will either increase your monthly EMI or extend your total loan repayment tenure.
