Home Loan Eligibility in Nepal: DTI Ratio, LTV Limits & How Banks Decide

Why your NPR 1.5 Crore home loan application got rejected despite a good salary - and the exact DTI and LTV math Nepal banks use to approve loans.

01. What is this?

When a Nepali bank evaluates your home loan application, it applies three key financial ratios. DTI (Debt-to-Income) compares your total monthly debt obligations to your gross income. LTV (Loan-to-Value) limits how much of the property value the bank will finance. DSCR (Debt Service Coverage Ratio) measures whether rental income from the property covers the loan EMI. Understanding these ratios lets you calculate your exact home loan eligibility before walking into a bank - and avoid the embarrassment and credit-score damage of a rejected application.

02. Why does it matter?

Nepal's housing loan market is tightly regulated by NRB. In 2079/80, NRB issued updated home loan circulars tightening LTV ratios particularly in Kathmandu Valley to control speculative real estate lending. Knowing these limits before you look at properties prevents you from falling in love with a house you cannot finance.

03. How does it work?

Step 01

Calculate Your Gross Monthly Income

Include all documented income: salary (get a bank statement or salary certificate), rental income (must have agreement), business income (last 2 years income tax returns required). Include only income you can prove - banks will not consider undocumented cash.

Step 02

Calculate Your DTI (EMI/Income Ratio)

Add up all existing monthly EMIs (vehicle loan, personal loan, gold loan, education loan). Banks allow a maximum 50-60% DTI. Formula: Max New EMI = Gross Income × 0.50 − Existing EMIs. If this number is negative, you need to pay off existing loans before applying for a home loan.

Step 03

Check NRB LTV Limits

NRB circulars set maximum LTV by property type and location. Kathmandu Valley residential: 50% LTV (bank finances max 50% of appraised value, you need 50% down payment). Outside Valley: up to 60% LTV. Self-occupied with income proof: may get up to 65% in some banks subject to internal credit policy.

Step 04

Use EMI Back-Calculation

Once you know max EMI (from DTI), back-calculate the loan amount. At 11% interest, 20-year tenure: every NPR 10,000 of monthly EMI capacity = approx NPR 10.5 Lakh loan. So if DTI allows NPR 30,000/month new EMI: max loan ≈ NPR 31.5 Lakh.

The Nepal Context & Regulatory Reality

05. How does it apply in Nepal?

In Nepal, real estate transaction prices are almost always under-reported ("black money" component) for stamp duty saving. Banks appraise at assessed (registered) value, not market value. This means your LTV calculation is based on the official registered price, not the actual amount you paid. Practically: if you pay NPR 80 Lakh for a property but register it at NPR 50 Lakh, the bank will lend up to 50% of NPR 50 Lakh = NPR 25 Lakh - even if the actual price was NPR 80 Lakh. This is a major mismatch that catches buyers off guard.

Regulatory Note: Statutory tax brackets, regulatory fees, and monetary caps reflect current official practice and are subject to periodic amendment in annual Nepal Finance Acts and NRB circulars. Always verify the latest gazetted directives before financial execution.

06. Practical Real-World Example in Nepal

Anita & Rohan, both 34, dual-income couple in Kathmandu

Combined NPR 1,40,000 / month (Anita NPR 65,000 + Rohan NPR 75,000)
Registration gap reduced loan eligibility from expected NPR 90L to actual NPR 30-35L
The Situation:

They wanted to buy a home in Bhaisepati at NPR 1.2 Crore (registered value NPR 60 Lakh). Rohan had an existing vehicle loan EMI of NPR 18,000. They expected to get NPR 90 Lakh home loan.

The RisePaisa Strategy:

NRB Calculation: LTV 50% on registered value NPR 60 Lakh = max loan NPR 30 Lakh. They needed NPR 1.2 Crore - NPR 30 Lakh = NPR 90 Lakh from own funds. Separately, DTI check: Max EMI = NPR 1,40,000 × 50% − NPR 18,000 (vehicle) = NPR 52,000 − NPR 18,000 = NPR 34,000. At NPR 34,000 EMI capacity and 11% interest for 20 years, max loan ≈ NPR 35.7 Lakh. Both constraints pointed to ~NPR 30-35 Lakh bank financing. They needed to increase registered value and down payment.

Sanjay's Home Loan Application: Solo Application vs Joint Spousal Income

Goal & Dilemma: Qualifying for an NPR 60,00,000 home loan (EMI ~NPR 58,000) on a solo salary of NPR 90,000.

Option A: Apply solo with verified salary of NPR 90,000 Rejected Under NRB DTI Rules

EMI of NPR 58,000 represents 64.4% DTI, exceeding NRB's mandatory 50% ceiling. The bank legally must reject or slash the loan to under NPR 45 Lakhs.

Option B: Apply jointly with his working spouse (combined salary NPR 140,000) Approved & Recommended

Combined DTI drops to a healthy 41.4%, safely within NRB's 50% regulatory limit, ensuring full sanction of the NPR 60 Lakh mortgage with preferential interest terms.

Decision Takeaway: Banks lend based on verified repayment capacity, not your dreams. Use joint income and keep existing debts zero to maximize your home loan eligibility.

07. Advantages & Limitations

Key Advantages
  • Mastering NRB Debt Service to Gross Income (DTI) ceilings (50-60%) ensures loan approval without repeated bank rejections.
  • Identifies legitimate income co-applicants (spouse, working parents) to dramatically expand borrowing capacity.
  • Prevents debt trap overleveraging where loan EMIs consume all disposable income, leading to lifestyle stress.
  • Clarifies the mandatory Loan-to-Value (LTV) limits for real estate financing inside and outside Kathmandu Valley.
Limitations & Risks
  • Banks strictly exclude informal cash business earnings or unverifiable side income from formal DTI calculations.
  • LTV ceiling in Kathmandu Valley is tightly restricted (typically 30% to 50% for commercial/residential purchases).
  • Existing credit card limits and personal vehicle EMIs reduce your available home loan borrowing headroom.
  • High application processing fees (0.5% to 1.0%) and legal valuation charges are non-refundable even if rejected.

08. Who Should Use This (And Who Should Avoid It)

Ideal For:
  • First-time prospective homebuyers preparing mortgage applications in Nepal.
  • Borrowers wanting to pre-calculate their maximum loan sanction amount before signing property token advances.
  • Couples pooling joint salaries to purchase an urban residential home or apartment.
Caution / Avoid If:
  • Individuals with existing bad debts, unsettled bouncing cheques, or CIB blacklisting.

09. Financial Formula & Step-by-Step Calculation

Mathematical Model

Home Loan Eligibility Formula

Max New EMI = (Gross Income × 0.50) - Existing EMIs

Variable Breakdown:

Gross Income
Total provable monthly income

All documented income - salary, rental, business.

0.50
DTI cap (50%)

NRB-guided maximum: total EMIs cannot exceed 50-60% of gross income.

Existing EMIs
All current loan repayments

Vehicle, personal, education, gold loan EMIs - every existing obligation.

Worked Nepal Scenario:

Gross income NPR 1,40,000. Existing vehicle EMI NPR 18,000. Max New EMI = 1,40,000 × 0.50 − 18,000 = 70,000 − 18,000 = NPR 52,000. At 11%/20 years: NPR 52,000 EMI capacity ≈ NPR 54.6 Lakh max loan.

10. Common Mistakes to Avoid in Nepal

Common Pitfall

Applying for a loan amount based on market property price, not registered value.

RisePaisa Best Practice

Banks lend against NRB-appraised or registered value (whichever is lower). Prepare down payment based on market price minus 50% of registered value.

Why: This gap between market and registered price is the #1 shock for first-time home buyers in Nepal.
Common Pitfall

Ignoring existing EMIs when estimating home loan eligibility.

RisePaisa Best Practice

Every existing EMI directly reduces your home loan capacity. Pay off smaller high-rate loans before applying.

Why: An NPR 18,000 vehicle EMI costs you ~NPR 19 Lakh in home loan eligibility.
Common Pitfall

Applying to only one bank.

RisePaisa Best Practice

Apply to 3-5 banks. Different banks have different internal policies on LTV and DTI within NRB limits. Some banks offer first-home-buyer incentives.

Why: Rate differences of 0.5% on a 20-year NPR 50 Lakh loan amount to NPR 3-4 Lakh in total interest difference.
