Loan Prepayment Math in Nepal: How an Extra NPR 5 Lakh Saves NPR 18 Lakh

Every single rupee of lump-sum prepayment goes 100% to reduce principal, shaving years off your mortgage and saving massive interest.

01. What is this?

Loan prepayment (कर्जाको आंशिक अग्रिम भुक्तानी) means paying an additional lump sum directly toward your loan's principal balance outside your regular monthly EMI. Because commercial bank loans in Nepal use reducing balance amortization, shrinking the principal immediately reduces the base upon which all future monthly interest is calculated.

02. Why does it matter?

On a standard NPR 50 Lakh, 20-year home loan at 11% interest, your monthly EMI is NPR 51,609. Over 20 years, you repay a staggering NPR 1.23 Crore - paying NPR 73.8 Lakh in pure interest! By making small lump-sum prepayments whenever you receive festive bonuses, savings, or business profits, you can destroy this interest burden and become debt-free a decade earlier.

03. How does it work?

Step 01

Analyze the Front-Loaded Amortization Curve

In year 1 of your 20-year loan, of your NPR 51,609 monthly EMI, roughly NPR 45,800 is interest and only NPR 5,800 is principal. Lenders collect their interest profit first.

Step 02

Execute a Direct Principal Prepayment

Deposit a lump sum (e.g., NPR 3-5 Lakh) into your loan account with a written instruction to the branch manager: "Credit directly to Principal Reduction (साँवा कट्टा)".

Step 03

Choose Tenure Reduction vs EMI Reduction

Keep your monthly EMI unchanged and request the bank to shorten the loan tenure. This maximizes total interest savings compared to lowering the EMI.

Step 04

Receive the Recalculated Amortization Schedule

Inspect your updated repayment schedule to verify that outstanding principal dropped immediately by the exact prepaid amount and remaining months shortened.

The Nepal Context & Regulatory Reality

05. How does it apply in Nepal?

Under Nepal Rastra Bank directives, banks are barred from charging exorbitant prepayment penalties on personal residential home loans up to NPR 50 Lakh (often zero or capped at 0.15% to 0.75% after a short initial period). Many borrowers receive annual festival allowances (Dashain bonus), gratuity, or dividends from investments and leave the cash in low-interest savings accounts earning 3%-4%, while paying 10%-12% on their home loan. Funneling excess cash into loan prepayment yields a guaranteed, tax-free return equal to your loan interest rate.

Regulatory Note: Statutory tax brackets, regulatory fees, and monetary caps reflect current official practice and are subject to periodic amendment in annual Nepal Finance Acts and NRB circulars. Always verify the latest gazetted directives before financial execution.

Bikash took an NPR 45 Lakh home loan for 15 years at 10.5% interest (EMI: NPR 49,830). In Year 2, he received a performance bonus of NPR 4 Lakh and considered buying a new motorcycle.

The RisePaisa Strategy:

That single prepayment shortened his 15-year loan by 2 years and 7 months, saving him NPR 11,40,000 in future interest payments. He realized prepayment was the highest-yielding risk-free investment available in Nepal.

07. Advantages & Limitations

Key Advantages
  • Every extra rupee prepaid directly slashes the loan principal, terminating years of compounding interest debt.
  • Prepaying early in a 20-year mortgage generates massive multi-lakh savings due to the front-loaded nature of bank amortization.
  • Shortens debt tenure dramatically without requiring large stressful lifestyle sacrifices.
  • Restores emotional freedom and complete asset ownership years ahead of contractual schedules.
Limitations & Risks
  • Prepaying funds locks cash into home equity, reducing personal emergency liquidity.
  • Opportunity cost if prepaid capital could earn higher post-tax returns in equity compounding (12%+) than loan interest (9-10%).
  • Certain commercial and corporate loans carry prepayment penalties (though capped by NRB for individuals).
  • Requires active coordination with the bank branch to ensure funds apply strictly to principal, not advance interest.

08. Who Should Use This (And Who Should Avoid It)

Ideal For:
  • Home loan and mortgage borrowers in years 1 to 7 of their repayment schedules.
  • Salaried professionals receiving annual Dashain festival bonuses or corporate performance bonuses.
  • Anyone carrying long-term bank debt wishing to become completely debt-free early.
Caution / Avoid If:
  • Borrowers who have zero emergency savings outside their loan.
  • Individuals whose loan interest rate is subsidized at ultra-low concessional rates (e.g. 4-5% subsidized agriculture loans).

09. Financial Formula & Step-by-Step Calculation

Mathematical Model

Total Interest Savings from Principal Prepayment

Savings = ∑t=1^{Torig} EMI - ( ∑t=1^{Tnew} EMI + Pprep )

Variable Breakdown:

Torig
Original Tenure (Months)

Initial repayment duration, e.g., 240 months.

Tnew
New Reduced Tenure (Months)

Shortened duration after applying prepayment to principal.

Pprep
Lump-Sum Prepayment Amount

E.g., NPR 5,00,000 paid directly toward principal.

Worked Nepal Scenario:

Original 240 months × NPR 51,609 = NPR 1,23,86,160. New 172 months × NPR 51,609 + NPR 5,00,000 prepay = NPR 93,76,748 + NPR 5,00,000 = NPR 98,76,748. Total Interest Saved = NPR 1,23,86,160 - NPR 98,76,748 = NPR 25,09,412 (over entire term)!

10. Common Mistakes to Avoid in Nepal

Common Pitfall

Choosing lower monthly EMI instead of tenure reduction when prepaying.

RisePaisa Best Practice

Always instruct the bank to shorten the loan tenure while keeping EMI constant.

Why: Shortening tenure compounds interest savings dramatically, whereas lowering EMI prolongs debt exposure.
Common Pitfall

Depositing cash into the savings account without an explicit letter to reduce principal.

RisePaisa Best Practice

Submit a formal written application specifying that the funds are for "Principal Curtailment" (साँवा कट्टा).

Why: Without instructions, banks may treat extra funds as advance EMIs, holding cash without stopping interest accrual.
Common Pitfall

Keeping large cash balances in savings at 3.5% while servicing a home loan at 11%.

RisePaisa Best Practice

Direct excess idle savings above your 6-month emergency reserve into loan principal prepayment.

Why: Paying off an 11% loan gives an instant, risk-free, 100% tax-free equivalent return of 11%.
