Bull Market and Bear Market

बुलिस र बेयरिस बजार (Bull & Bear Market)
Plain-Language Definition

A Bull Market is a sustained financial period of rising stock prices and investor optimism; a Bear Market is a prolonged period of declining asset prices by 20% or more.

Detailed Explanation

Stock markets move in secular macroeconomic cycles driven by banking liquidity, interest rate spreads, corporate earnings, and investor psychology. On NEPSE, a Bull Market is characterized by falling bank interest rates, expanding daily trading turnovers (e.g., reaching NPR 10 to 20 Arba per day), widespread participation, and consistent series of higher highs and higher lows. Conversely, a Bear Market occurs when banking liquidity tightens, interest rates surge, daily turnover contracts dramatically, and index values decline persistently over months or years.

Why It Matters to You

Understanding where NEPSE sits in its macro cycle prevents investors from aggressively buying at the peak of euphoric bull bubbles or panic-selling at the bottom of prolonged bear market capitulations.

Where It Is Commonly Used

  • Macroeconomic market cycle analysis
  • Asset allocation between equities and fixed deposits
  • Risk management strategies

Key Advantages & Limitations

Key Advantages

  • Bull markets generate rapid wealth appreciation
  • Bear markets provide once-in-a-decade bargain buying opportunities

Limitations & Risks

  • Emotional investing leads to buying at market tops and selling at market bottoms

Common Misconceptions & Beginner Traps

Myth

A bull market will continue rising forever.

Reality

Every financial market in the world is cyclical. Bull runs eventually end when valuations detach from earnings or monetary liquidity tightens.

Nepal Practical Context

Nepal Financial & Regulatory Context

Historically, NEPSE cycles are intensely tied to commercial bank liquidity and Nepal Rastra Bank monetary policies. Major historical bull runs peaked at 1,175 (2008), 1,881 (2016), and 3,198 (2021).

Regulatory Note: Statutory rules, tax brackets, and official limits are subject to periodic amendment in annual Nepal Finance Acts and NRB circulars. Always verify current gazetted regulations.

Real-World Practical Scenario

The Scenario:

Investor strategy during a NEPSE Bear Market. NEPSE drops from 3,200 to 1,800 over 18 months. Panic sellers dump fundamentally strong banks at P/E under 12x. Disciplined investor maintains monthly SIP and dollar-cost averages. When market enters next bull cycle, accumulated low-cost units deliver 2x to 3x returns

Key Takeaway:

Patience and long-term horizon convert bear market discounts into wealth creation.
