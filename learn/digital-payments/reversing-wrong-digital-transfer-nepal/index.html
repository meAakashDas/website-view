How to Recover Money Sent to the Wrong Wallet or Bank Account in Nepal

Immediate step-by-step emergency protocol for retrieving funds transferred to the wrong mobile number or bank account via connectIPS, Fonepay, eSewa, or Khalti under Nepali law.

01. What is this?

A Misdirected Digital Transfer occurs when a user accidentally enters an incorrect bank account number, mistypes a 10-digit mobile wallet number, or selects the wrong contact from their transaction history, causing funds to be credited to an unintended third party. Because digital payment rails in Nepal operate on real-time settlement architectures managed by Nepal Clearing House Limited (NCHL) and Fonepay, funds leave your account within seconds. However, Nepali law and banking regulations provide strict dispute resolution mechanisms to recover mistakenly transferred funds.

02. Why does it matter?

Every day in Nepal, hundreds of thousands of rupees are mistakenly sent to wrong phone numbers due to typos. Many people panic, assume the money is lost forever, or beg the wrong recipient who promptly blocks their phone calls. In Nepal, keeping money that does not belong to you is illegal under Section 249 of the National Penal Code 2074 (Criminal Misappropriation). Knowing the exact administrative and legal levers allows you to freeze the money before the recipient withdraws or spends it.

03. How does it work?

Step 01

Document the Transaction Immediately

Take high-resolution screenshots of the success screen showing: Transaction Reference ID (TXN ID), Amount, Date & Time, and the wrong account/mobile number.

Step 02

Contact Your Bank / Wallet Customer Care

Call your bank or wallet helpline (eSewa 1660-01-02121 / Khalti 1660-01-58888 / connectIPS 01-4265306) and state: "I made an erroneous transfer; please initiate an urgent Dispute Recall Request."

Step 03

Inter-Bank Freezing Request (Lien)

Your bank sends an official electronic Chargeback / Reversal Request via the NCHL dispute portal to the recipient’s bank, requesting a temporary freeze on the disputed funds.

Step 04

Recipient Verification & Consent

The receiving bank contacts the wrong recipient to obtain verbal or written debit consent to reverse the funds back to your account.

Step 05

Police Complaint if Recipient Refuses

If the recipient blocks calls or spends the money, file a formal complaint at the nearest District Police Office or Nepal Police Cyber Bureau for Criminal Misappropriation.

The Nepal Context & Regulatory Reality

05. How does it apply in Nepal?

Under Nepal Rastra Bank Unified Directives on Payment Systems, all payment service providers (PSPs) and payment system operators (PSOs) must maintain a dedicated 24/7 dispute resolution desk. Furthermore, under Section 15 of the Banking Offence and Punishment Act 2064, unauthorized withdrawal or retention of funds credited mistakenly constitutes a banking offense punishable by full recovery, equal fines, and up to 3 years imprisonment.

Regulatory Note: Statutory tax brackets, regulatory fees, and monetary caps reflect current official practice and are subject to periodic amendment in annual Nepal Finance Acts and NRB circulars. Always verify the latest gazetted directives before financial execution.

06. Practical Real-World Example in Nepal

Kripa, 24, university student in Butwal

Allowance: NPR 12,000 / month
Recovered NPR 15,000 in 4 days by initiating an official bank dispute recall within 10 minutes
The Situation:

Kripa intended to transfer NPR 15,000 to her college fee account via connectIPS. In a rush, she mistyped the final two digits of the account number, sending the money to an unknown stranger at another commercial bank in Biratnagar.

The RisePaisa Strategy:

Instead of panicking, Kripa took immediate action: (1) She saved the connectIPS TXN ID. (2) She called her bank helpline within 10 minutes and filed an "Erroneous Transfer Dispute". (3) Her bank flagged the transaction to NCHL, which immediately alerted the recipient’s bank in Biratnagar to place a temporary lien on the NPR 15,000. (4) The Biratnagar bank branch manager called the account holder, explained that the funds were sent by mistake and that refusing to return them carried legal penalties under the Banking Offence Act. The recipient signed a reversal consent, and Kripa received her full NPR 15,000 back in her account within 4 days.

07. Advantages & Limitations

Key Advantages
  • Provides clear legal protocols under the Banking Offence Act 2064 to recover mistakenly transferred funds.
  • Empowers sending banks to initiate electronic inter-bank liens (freezes) via NCHL within hours.
  • Educates users on dispute desk helplines across connectIPS, Fonepay, eSewa, and Khalti.
  • Eliminates panic by establishing a systematic documentation trail (TXN ID, timestamp, return memo).
Limitations & Risks
  • Transfers cannot be unilaterally reversed by the sender directly in-app once authorized.
  • If the recipient withdraws the cash at an ATM before a bank lien is placed, recovery requires police litigation.
  • Banks cannot debit a third party’s account without their written/verbal consent or an official police order.
  • Inter-bank disputes typically take 2 to 5 business days to clear.

08. Who Should Use This (And Who Should Avoid It)

Ideal For:
  • Anyone who mistyped an account or phone number during mobile banking, connectIPS, or digital wallet transfers.
  • Digital banking users wanting to learn proactive preventative habits (like the NPR 10 test transfer).
Caution / Avoid If:
  • Victims of advance-fee lottery scams where fraudsters immediately siphon funds abroad (requires Cyber Bureau fraud filing).

10. Common Mistakes to Avoid in Nepal

Common Pitfall

Calling the wrong recipient directly and aggressively accusing them of theft.

RisePaisa Best Practice

Let your bank or wallet operator contact the recipient formally through banking channels.

Why: Aggressive personal calls often cause recipients to panic, get defensive, and switch off their phones.
Common Pitfall

Waiting days before reporting the erroneous transfer to your bank.

RisePaisa Best Practice

Report within minutes; if the recipient withdraws the cash at an ATM, recovery becomes protracted.

Why: Banks can only freeze funds that are still present in the recipient’s account balance.

12. Knowledge Check & Review Questions

Under standard banking privacy laws, banks require either the recipient’s written/verbal consent OR a formal investigation letter from the Nepal Police / Court order to execute the reversal.

Visit your nearest District Police Office with your transaction receipt. The police will issue an official directive to the recipient’s bank to freeze all their other accounts and trace their citizenship and registered address.

Always use the NPR 10 test transfer protocol: send NPR 10 first to verify the recipient’s registered full name before transferring the large balance.
