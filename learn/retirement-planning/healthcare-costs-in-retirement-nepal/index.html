Planning for Medical & Healthcare Expenses in Retirement in Nepal

Prepare for Nepal’s steep 10%-12% healthcare inflation: optimizing the government Swasthya Beema Board program, supplemental private critical illness cover, and building an untouchable senior medical buffer.

01. What is this?

Retirement Healthcare Planning is the proactive financial strategy of funding medical treatments, prescription medications, specialized diagnostic scans, and long-term geriatric care during old age without depleting your retirement nest egg or burdening your children. In Nepal, where public healthcare infrastructure is congested and high-quality tertiary care is concentrated in private hospitals (such as Mediciti, Grande, Teaching Hospital, and Bharatpur Cancer Hospital), out-of-pocket medical expenditures account for over 55% of all healthcare spending in the country.

02. Why does it matter?

Most Nepalis calculate their retirement corpus based solely on food, rent, and utility bills, assuming their living costs will decrease as they age. In reality, while clothing and travel expenses decline after age 60, healthcare expenditures explode exponentially. A retiree with an otherwise healthy NPR 50 Lakh retirement corpus can see 40% of their life savings wiped out by a single 2-week ICU hospitalization for a stroke or pneumonia. Without a dedicated medical fund and layered insurance, healthcare costs become the number one cause of elderly poverty in Nepal.

03. How does it work?

Step 01

Enroll in the Government Health Insurance Program

Register your family with the Health Insurance Board (HIB / स्वास्थ्य बीमा बोर्ड) via your local municipal ward office. Pay NPR 3,500 annually to secure up to NPR 1,00,000 basic coverage (free for senior citizens aged 70+).

Step 02

Purchase Private Critical Illness Cover Before Age 50

Secure a Critical Illness (घातक रोग) policy covering 15-20 major diseases (cancer, stroke, kidney failure) while you are healthy. Pre-existing conditions after age 55 make buying private insurance nearly impossible.

Step 03

Establish a Dedicated Senior Medical Buffer

Do not mix your medical fund with daily living money. Lock NPR 10 Lakh to NPR 15 Lakh in a 3-month rolling Fixed Deposit in a Class A bank designated strictly for hospital emergencies.

Step 04

Budget for Monthly Chronic Medications

Calculate ongoing costs for hypertension, diabetes, thyroid, and cholesterol medicines (typically NPR 3,000 to NPR 8,000/month) directly into your baseline retirement cash flow.

Step 05

Nominate a Healthcare Power of Attorney

Ensure your adult children or trusted nominee have legal authorization to access the medical bank account through dual-signatory access or digital banking mandates during emergencies.

The Nepal Context & Regulatory Reality

05. How does it apply in Nepal?

Under the Senior Citizens Act 2063 (ज्येष्ठ नागरिक ऐन) and National Health Policy, the Government of Nepal provides free health insurance coverage up to NPR 1,00,000 for citizens aged 70 and above, along with mandatory 50% discounts on hospital beds and medications in government hospitals. However, due to severe shortages of specialized medicines and diagnostic equipment in government facilities, retirees frequently need substantial cash reserves to access private healthcare.

Regulatory Note: Statutory tax brackets, regulatory fees, and monetary caps reflect current official practice and are subject to periodic amendment in annual Nepal Finance Acts and NRB circulars. Always verify the latest gazetted directives before financial execution.

06. Practical Real-World Example in Nepal

Krishna, 64, retired bank officer in Kathmandu

Retirement corpus: NPR 60 Lakh; Monthly SSF/CIT pension: NPR 35,000
Protected his entire retirement nest egg and family peace of mind through a dedicated medical buffer
The Situation:

Krishna believed his NPR 35,000 pension was sufficient for life. At age 66, he suffered a sudden cardiac episode requiring emergency angioplasty and two coronary stents at a private hospital in Lalitpur. The total bill reached NPR 9,50,000.

The RisePaisa Strategy:

Because Krishna had established a three-layer healthcare plan before retiring: (1) His government health insurance covered NPR 1,00,000 of initial diagnostics. (2) He pulled the remaining NPR 8,50,000 from his dedicated "Senior Medical FD" without touching his primary NPR 60 Lakh retirement nest egg or selling any shares at a market loss. His monthly pension continued uninterrupted, and his children did not have to take emergency loans.

07. Advantages & Limitations

Key Advantages
  • Protects the core retirement nest egg from being wiped out by a single catastrophic private hospital admission.
  • Maximizes government subsidies: up to NPR 1,00,000 annual coverage via Swasthya Beema Board (free for seniors 70+).
  • Ensures medical dignity and independence in old age without relying on financially stressed adult children.
  • Accounts for high 10%-12% healthcare inflation in urban private hospitals (Kathmandu, Pokhara, Chitwan).
Limitations & Risks
  • Private health insurance policies exclude pre-existing chronic conditions (hypertension, diabetes) if purchased after age 60.
  • Government health insurance requires visiting designated public referral hospitals with frequent medicine shortages.
  • Maintaining a separate liquid NPR 10-15 Lakh medical buffer requires diligent pre-retirement capital allocation.
  • Home nursing and specialized palliative care remain largely uninsurable private expenses in Nepal.

08. Who Should Use This (And Who Should Avoid It)

Ideal For:
  • Individuals aged 40 to 60 planning their retirement corpus allocation in Nepal.
  • Adult children wanting to establish an independent medical safety net for their aging parents.
Caution / Avoid If:
  • Young starters in their 20s who need primary term life and basic emergency funds first.

10. Common Mistakes to Avoid in Nepal

Common Pitfall

Assuming your adult children will easily afford your private ICU and surgery bills.

RisePaisa Best Practice

Build your own self-funded medical buffer to maintain medical dignity and prevent burdening children.

Why: Young couples in Nepal often face high mortgage and schooling costs and cannot easily absorb a NPR 10 Lakh medical emergency.
Common Pitfall

Waiting until age 60 to buy private health or critical illness insurance.

RisePaisa Best Practice

Purchase critical illness insurance in your 30s or 40s before diabetes or hypertension develops.

Why: Insurance companies in Nepal outright exclude pre-existing conditions or reject senior applicants.
