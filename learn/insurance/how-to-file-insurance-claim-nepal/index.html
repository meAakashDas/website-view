Step-by-Step Insurance Claim Process in Nepal: Avoiding Rejections

From 24-hour hospitalization intimation to gathering itemized discharge summaries, lab reports, and appealing unfair insurer repudiations.

01. What is this?

An insurance claim is the formal contractual request submitted by a policyholder to their insurance company demanding compensation for a covered medical hospitalization, critical illness diagnosis, property damage, or death. In Nepal, claim settlement is governed by strict regulatory timelines set by the Nepal Insurance Authority (NIA / नेपाल बिमा प्राधिकरण).

02. Why does it matter?

Paying insurance premiums for years is pointless if your claim is rejected during a medical emergency because you lost an original pharmacy bill or reported the admission 4 days too late. Understanding the exact evidentiary checklist ensures your claim is settled smoothly within the statutory 21-to-35-day regulatory settlement window.

03. How does it work?

Step 01

Send Prompt Claim Intimation (Within 24-48 Hours)

As soon as the patient is admitted to the hospital, email the insurance company or call their toll-free claims desk. State the patient's name, policy number, hospital name, and tentative diagnosis.

Step 02

Collect Original Documents at Discharge

Collect: (1) Complete Discharge Summary (Chhutti Purji) detailing admission/discharge dates and treatment history, (2) Original itemized hospital bills with cash counter stamps, (3) Doctor's written prescriptions for all medicines, (4) All diagnostic reports (Blood, X-Ray, CT/MRI).

Step 03

Submit Complete Dossier Within 15-30 Days

Fill out the official Claim Form. Attach your PAN, citizenship, and bank cheque photo for direct connectIPS settlement. Submit the complete physical packet to the insurer's branch and secure a stamped acknowledgment receipt.

Step 04

Review Settlement & Escalate Disputes

The surveyor/medical officer assesses the claim. Upon approval, funds are wired to your bank. If the insurer unjustly repudiates the claim, submit a formal petition to the Complaints Department at Nepal Insurance Authority (Kupandole, Lalitpur).

The Nepal Context & Regulatory Reality

05. How does it apply in Nepal?

Under Nepal Insurance Authority Directives, once a policyholder submits all complete required documents, the insurer is legally mandated to settle or formally repudiate the claim within 35 days. If the company delays settlement beyond this window without valid regulatory cause, they are liable to pay statutory interest on the delayed claim amount.

Regulatory Note: Statutory tax brackets, regulatory fees, and monetary caps reflect current official practice and are subject to periodic amendment in annual Nepal Finance Acts and NRB circulars. Always verify the latest gazetted directives before financial execution.

06. Practical Real-World Example in Nepal

Laxmi, 38, high school teacher in Biratnagar

NPR 50,000 / month salary
Recovered NPR 1,48,000 on a rejected claim by reconstructing itemized hospital records
The Situation:

Laxmi underwent an emergency laparoscopic gallbladder surgery costing NPR 1,60,000. She threw away her daily pharmacy receipts, submitting only the hospital's single-page summary invoice to her insurer, which promptly rejected her claim.

The RisePaisa Strategy:

Her insurance advisor guided her back to the hospital: the billing department reprinted duplicate itemized pharmacy bills and diagnostic reports with official hospital seals. She resubmitted the complete dossier with an explanation letter. The insurer approved NPR 1,48,000 (after deducting NPR 12,000 in non-medical PPE and admission charges) and transferred the cash via connectIPS within 18 days.

Decision Takeaway: Insurance claims succeed or fail on documentation. Keep your original policy, nominee citizenship, and hospital records organized in a single home emergency folder.

07. Advantages & Limitations

Key Advantages
  • Demystifies the insurance claim lifecycle from immediate incident intimation to final settlement bank transfer.
  • Prepares all mandatory statutory documentation (Death Certificate, Ward Sarjameen, Hospital Discharge Summary, Police FIR).
  • Explains the legal dispute resolution mechanism under the Nepal Insurance Authority (NIA) for rejected or delayed claims.
  • Eliminates reliance on commission-hungry middlemen, ensuring direct settlement into the legal nominee's account.
Limitations & Risks
  • Missing paperwork or incomplete hospital clinical notes lead to frustrating claim settlement delays.
  • Strict statutory intimation deadlines (typically within 15 to 35 days of death or incident).
  • Non-disclosure of pre-existing medical conditions during initial policy signing is the #1 cause of total claim rejection.
  • Suicide within the first 1-2 years of policy inception is legally excluded from death benefit payouts.

08. Who Should Use This (And Who Should Avoid It)

Ideal For:
  • Nominees and family members needing to claim life or medical insurance in Nepal.
  • Policyholders preparing an emergency claims checklist for their spouses and dependents.
  • Anyone experiencing unfair claim repudiation or prolonged delay by an insurer.
Caution / Avoid If:
  • Individuals filing fraudulent claims with fabricated medical receipts (punishable by imprisonment under the Insurance Act).

09. Financial Formula & Step-by-Step Calculation

Mathematical Model

Net Insurance Claim Reimbursement Formula

Net Payout = Total Hospital Bill - Non-Medical Items - Co-Payment (if any) - Room Cap Deductions

Variable Breakdown:

Total Hospital Bill
Gross Billed Amount

Total invoice presented by the treating hospital in Nepalese Rupees.

Non-Medical Items
Excluded Sundries

Admission charges, PPE kits, sanitizers, thermometer, deluxe food.

Co-Payment
Mandatory Cost Share

Agreed policy deductible percentage (typically 10% to 15% on senior policies).

Room Cap Deductions
Proportionate Penalty

Deductions applied if the selected room exceeded policy bed ceilings.

Worked Nepal Scenario:

Total bill = NPR 2,00,000. Non-medical exclusions = NPR 15,000. Eligible expenses = NPR 1,85,000. Policy has a 10% co-payment clause: Co-payment = 1,85,000 * 0.10 = NPR 18,500. Net insurance payout wired to bank = 1,85,000 - 18,500 = NPR 1,66,500.

10. Common Mistakes to Avoid in Nepal

Common Pitfall

Throwing away individual pharmacy cash slips and submitting only a summary bill.

RisePaisa Best Practice

Preserve every single individual medicine receipt with its corresponding doctor prescription.

Why: Insurers reject bulk pharmacy amounts without itemized proof of medicine purchase.
Common Pitfall

Waiting 2 months after discharge before notifying the insurance company.

RisePaisa Best Practice

Intimate the company within 48 hours of admission and submit files within 15-30 days.

Why: Late intimation violates policy contract conditions and provides grounds for claim repudiation.
Common Pitfall

Accepting claim rejection verbally over the phone without a formal letter.

RisePaisa Best Practice

Demand an official written claim repudiation letter citing the exact policy clause.

Why: A written rejection letter is mandatory to file an appeal with the Nepal Insurance Authority.

12. Knowledge Check & Review Questions

Cashless facility is available only if your insurer has a formal Third-Party Administrator (TPA) tie-up with that specific hospital. Otherwise, you pay cash and file for reimbursement.

Submit a formal written complaint with your policy copy and rejection letter to the Complaints Handling Division at Nepal Insurance Authority (Beema Pradhikaran), Kupandole, Lalitpur.

Under official Beema Pradhikaran guidelines, insurers must settle complete claims within 21 to 35 days of receiving all verified documents.

Under Nepal Insurance Authority directives, an insurance company must settle or reject a claim with written justification within 35 days of receiving all necessary documents from the claimant.

You can file a formal complaint with the Grievance Handling Cell of the Nepal Insurance Authority (नेपाल बिमा प्राधिकरण, Kupondole, Lalitpur), which possesses statutory judicial powers to adjudicate claims.
