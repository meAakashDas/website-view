SIP Investing in Nepal: Complete Guide to Mutual Funds & Compounding

Written by Aakash Das · Published: May 4, 2026 · Verified: Oct 18, 2026 · 10 min read

For salaried professionals, young workers, and college students in Nepal who do not have the time to track NEPSE daily or analyze balance sheets, direct stock trading can feel overwhelming. Fortunately, SEBON’s modernization of the mutual fund industry has made Systematic Investment Plans (SIP - व्यवस्थित लगानी योजना) one of the most effective, stress-free ways to build long-term wealth in Nepal.

With an SIP, you invest a fixed sum of money (starting from as little as NPR 1,000 per month) into a SEBON-regulated open-ended mutual fund. This guide explains how SIPs operate, why Rupee Cost Averaging protects you during stock market downturns, and how to start your first plan online.

1. Open-Ended vs Closed-Ended Mutual Funds in Nepal

To understand SIPs, you must distinguish between the two mutual fund structures in Nepal:

  • Closed-Ended Mutual Funds: These have a fixed fund size and a fixed maturity lifespan (typically 5, 7, or 10 years). They issue units via an initial public offering, list on NEPSE, and are traded like ordinary stocks through brokers. You cannot start an automated monthly SIP in a closed-ended fund.
  • Open-Ended Mutual Funds: These schemes do not have a fixed maturity date or fixed unit limits. Units are bought and sold directly through the Fund Manager (Merchant Capital) at the daily published Net Asset Value (NAV - खुद सम्पत्ति मूल्य). These are the schemes that facilitate automated SIPs.

2. The Magic of Rupee Cost Averaging

The single greatest psychological advantage of an SIP is that it eliminates the dangerous urge to "time the market." When markets drop, retail investors often panic and stop investing. An SIP turns market volatility into your biggest advantage through Rupee Cost Averaging:

Month Monthly SIP (NPR) Scheme NAV (NPR) Units Allotted Investor Advantage
Month 1 NPR 5,000 NPR 10.00 500.00 Units Baseline initial investment
Month 2 (Market Drops) NPR 5,000 NPR 8.00 625.00 Units You automatically buy MORE units at a discount!
Month 3 (Market Recovers) NPR 5,000 NPR 12.50 400.00 Units Total units appreciate significantly

Across these 3 months, you invested a total of NPR 15,000 and accumulated 1,525 units. Your average cost per unit was NPR 15,000 / 1,525 = NPR 9.83—well below the original NAV of NPR 10.00 and far below the peak of NPR 12.50. When the market surges, your accumulated low-cost units generate compound returns.

3. The Compounding Engine: Dividend Reinvestment Plan (DREP)

When enrolling in an SIP in Nepal, fund managers provide two options for annual dividend distributions:

  1. Cash Dividend Payout: Annual fund returns are credited in cash to your bank account.
  2. Dividend Reinvestment Plan (DREP): Your declared annual dividend is automatically used to purchase additional units of the fund at prevailing NAV with zero entry fees.

For investors with a 10- to 20-year time horizon, choosing DREP unleashes true exponential compounding. Reinvesting dividends ensures that each subsequent year's returns generate further returns on an ever-expanding base of units.

4. Popular Open-Ended Mutual Funds in Nepal

Prominent SEBON-regulated open-ended mutual fund schemes currently operating in Nepal include:

  • NIBL Sahabhagita Fund: Managed by NIMB Ace Capital (Nepal's pioneer open-ended scheme).
  • Siddhartha Systematic Investment Scheme: Managed by Siddhartha Capital.
  • Kumari Sunaulo Yojana: Managed by Kumari Capital.
  • NIC Asia Dynamic Debt / Growth Fund: Managed by NIC Asia Capital.
  • Nabil Flexi Cap Fund: Managed by Nabil Investment Banking.

5. How to Start Your SIP Online (Step-by-Step)

  1. Prerequisites: Ensure you have your 16-digit DEMAT BOID, active bank account, and ConnectIPS or mobile wallet account ready.
  2. Visit the Capital Portal: Navigate to the official website of the merchant banker managing the open-ended scheme of your choice.
  3. Complete SIP Registration: Click on "SIP Registration". Enter your DEMAT account details, select the scheme, and designate your preferred installment amount (e.g., NPR 1,000, NPR 2,500, or NPR 5,000).
  4. Choose Frequency & Duration: Select monthly or quarterly deduction intervals and specify your desired term (e.g., 5 years, 10 years, or perpetual).
  5. Set Up Auto-Debit via ConnectIPS: Link your bank account using ConnectIPS Mandate/e-Mandate so monthly contributions are debited automatically without manual intervention.
